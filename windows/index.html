Messages for Windows

A native Windows 11 app with all four services in one inbox. On its own it needs no server.

Coming soon to the Microsoft Store. It isn't available to download yet. This page will link to the Store listing once it's up.

What you need

  • A Windows 11 PC (x64). Everything Messages needs ships inside it, so there's nothing to install first.
  • The accounts you want to connect. For Google Messages, your Android phone with Google Messages on it.

First run

Messages opens on a welcome screen with three ways in:

  • Connect your accounts opens Settings, Accounts.
  • Use a Messages Server you have joins a server that keeps your accounts connected at home.
  • Look around with sample conversations shows Messages with made-up people. Nothing is sent, and your own data stays closed. Leave sample in the banner takes you back.

Connecting your accounts

In Settings (Ctrl+,), Accounts has a card for each service.

Google Messages
Choose Pair. On your phone, open Google Messages, tap your account picture, then Device pairing. Sign in with the same Google account in the window that opens, then tap the emoji on your phone that matches the one Messages shows.
Messenger
Choose Connect, sign in to Facebook in the window that opens, then choose Done. Encrypted chats turn on by themselves.
Instagram
Choose Connect and sign in with your Instagram username and password. Log in with Facebook isn't supported.
Google Voice
Choose Add Google Voice account and sign in at voice.google.com. Add more numbers with Add another Google Voice account.

The sign-in windows are the services' own pages. Messages never types your password for you.

Living in the tray

  • Closing the window leaves Messages running in the system tray, so new messages still reach you. Quit Messages in the tray icon's menu closes it.
  • After your first account connects, Messages asks once whether to start when you sign in to Windows.
  • F1 opens the full guide inside the app, and Ctrl+/ lists the keyboard shortcuts.

Using a Messages Server from this PC

If you run a Messages Server, the PC can use it instead of its own sign-ins. Open Settings, Accounts, Messages Server, Manage…, then one of these:

  • Paste the line from the server's page. Its Add a PC step shows one line that holds everything the PC needs.
  • Ask to join. Enter the server's address and port, like server.lan:8787. The PC shows a short code; approve it on the server's page only if the code there is the same. You're then given a finish code to type on the PC.

Once it's connected, Move to this server… puts the PC's accounts and choices on the server. Messenger and Instagram move as they are, with no new sign-in. Google Messages and Google Voice are signed in on the server itself.

Away from home, a PC with the Tailscale app installed can use the server's Tailscale name, messages-server, as its address.

A phone too Coming

A later version of Messages for Windows runs Messages Server on the PC itself, so your phone can use the accounts connected there:

  • It starts when you sign in to Windows and runs as your own user, not as a Windows service. The phone gets messages while that PC is on and you're signed in.
  • The phone reaches it through Tailscale, as with any other server.
  • Its backups can go to any folder you can write to, a mapped drive, a network share or OneDrive included.

Until then, the Android app needs a server on Unraid, Proxmox, Docker or Linux, and this PC can use the same one.
